## I had two broad ideas: 

1) Make some sort of a tool or system for Students (aged 12-15) to be able to log their academic interests overtime and think critically about these areas of interest. 
2) Do something in the crochet/fiber arts/crafts space 
		Either an interaction design project (Projection mapping etc)
		Or, a tool that helps with pattern creation (Specifically for fiber artists)


## Exploring idea 1)

###### What I want to do 
I want to explore the need for a tool/medium that allows students aged 12-15 to track their interests, whether academic or extracurricular. It can really be used any time they want, but the hope is that, at the end of each academic year, class teachers and/or school counsellors can lead a class session to facilitate its use. ###### Visuals
Classmates can see streams form on the screen, all intersecting with each other - tributaries and distributaries (Kind of what you’d see on a physical map of a state/country with its water bodies). Nodes will form, and the teacher/facilitator will have data on overlaps and potential groups of students who can sit together to discuss their interests.

###### More thoughts/goals 
Not an aptitude test. More of a data viz output that lays out, clearly, what one’s interests are. “Stream” colloquially in India is a way to say what subjects you’d pick after the 10th grade - Science, Humanities, and Commerce. 

I think there is an overlap between STEM and the arts (at least). Physics and philosophy were once inextricably linked. There’s always been an overlap. 

Streams/rivers have distributaries and tributaries that all meet, and I think this is a great way to view academic interests. It’ll cultivate a healthier, holistic mindset towards what the student eventually pursues. 

Growing up, I always had a curiosity about science and tech that wasn’t nurtured by my environment - and by extension - me. That curiosity turned to resentment - rotted and died an insignificant death. Then I moved to my natural inclination and “aptitude” in the arts - literature, media, etc., and grieved my dead curiosity about science. Now I’m trying to make my way in a field that I like to believe thrives at the intersection of all my interests, and I don’t feel claustrophobic in my identity as a STEM or Arts person (?)
